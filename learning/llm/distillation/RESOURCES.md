# Distillation Resources

## Knowledge

### Foundations

- [Paper: "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network", Hinton, Vinyals and Dean, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
  The paper that named the method: soft targets, temperature, the T squared gradient correction and the combined loss, with MNIST and speech experiments. Use for: stage 1, from the source.
- [Paper: "A Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models", Xu et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13116)
  A map of LLM distillation organised by what is elicited from the teacher and how the student is trained on it. Use for: placing a method in the wider family before reading its own paper.
- [Paper: "DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter", Sanh et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01108)
  A worked distillation of a transformer during pretraining with a triple loss of distillation, masked language modelling and cosine embedding terms. Use for: a complete recipe with an ablation of each loss term.

### Logit and sequence-level distillation

- [Paper: "Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation", Kim and Rush, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07947)
  Word-level against sequence-level distillation for translation, and training on the teacher's beam-search output. Use for: stages 2 and 3, the original framing of both levels.
- [Paper: "MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models", Gu et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543)
  The case for reverse KL in generative distillation, optimised with a policy-gradient method on student samples. Use for: forward against reverse KL, and one form of on-policy distillation.
- [Paper: "Towards Cross-Tokenizer Distillation: the Universal Logit Distillation Loss for LLMs", Boizard et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12030)
  A loss based on optimal transport that compares sorted probability vectors so teacher and student need not share a vocabulary. Use for: what cross-tokenizer distillation does and gives up.
- [Paper: "Gemma 2: Improving Open Language Models at a Practical Size", Gemma Team, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118)
  Training the 2B and 9B models with knowledge distillation from a larger teacher instead of next-token prediction. Use for: distillation as a pretraining signal.
- [Paper: "Compact Language Models via Pruning and Knowledge Distillation", Muralidharan et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14679)
  Minitron: pruning a trained model and retraining it with distillation from the original, on a small fraction of the original training tokens. Use for: distillation as the recovery step after pruning.
- [Paper: "LLM Pruning and Distillation in Practice: The Minitron Approach", Sreenivas et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11796)
  The Minitron recipe applied to Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral NeMo 12B, including correcting the teacher on the distillation data first. Use for: a second, more recent run of the same recipe.

### Synthetic-data distillation

- [Paper: "Self-Instruct: Aligning Language Models with Self-Generated Instructions", Wang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)
  Bootstrapping instruction data from a model's own generations, with filtering for near-duplicates and invalid examples. Use for: how a generated instruction set is built.
- [Article: "Alpaca: A Strong, Replicable Instruction-Following Model", Stanford CRFM, 2023](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2023/03/13/alpaca.html)
  Fine-tuning LLaMA 7B on 52K instructions generated by text-davinci-003 with the Self-Instruct method, its cost, and its stated limits and license restrictions. Use for: the canonical cheap imitation run.
- [Paper: "Distilling Step-by-Step! Outperforming Larger Language Models with Less Training Data and Smaller Model Sizes", Hsieh et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02301)
  Training a small model to produce the teacher's rationales as a second task alongside its labels. Use for: what rationales add over labels alone.
- [Paper: "Orca: Progressive Learning from Complex Explanation Traces of GPT-4", Mukherjee et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02707)
  Imitating explanation traces elicited with system prompts, with ChatGPT as an intermediate teacher before GPT-4. Use for: explanation traces and a teacher assistant at LLM scale.
- [Paper: "Textbooks Are All You Need", Gunasekar et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644)
  phi-1: a small code model trained on filtered web data and synthetic textbooks and exercises generated by GPT-3.5. Use for: data quality against data volume.
- [Paper: "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning", DeepSeek-AI, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)
  Distilling a reasoning model into smaller Qwen and Llama models by fine-tuning on its curated samples, compared with running RL on the small model directly (appendix F.1). Use for: rejection-sampled synthetic data, and the post-training lesson this track links to.

### On-policy distillation

- [Paper: "On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes", Agarwal et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649)
  Generalized knowledge distillation: training on student-generated sequences with a choice of divergence and a mix of fixed and on-policy data. Use for: stage 5, from the source.
- [Paper: "Qwen3 Technical Report", Qwen Team, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)
  Strong-to-weak distillation of the small Qwen3 models, with an off-policy stage followed by an on-policy stage, compared against RL on the same models. Use for: on-policy distillation in a production model family.
- [Documentation: "Generalized Knowledge Distillation Trainer", Hugging Face TRL](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/gkd_trainer)
  The TRL implementation of GKD and its `lmbda`, `beta` and `seq_kd` parameters. Use for: running on-policy distillation rather than reading about it.
- [Documentation: "General Online Logit Distillation (GOLD) Trainer", Hugging Face TRL](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/gold_trainer)
  TRL's trainer that extends GKD with the Universal Logit Distillation loss, aligning text spans so teacher and student may use different tokenizers. Use for: what a cross-tokenizer on-policy run needs.

### What the student inherits

- [Paper: "Does Knowledge Distillation Really Work?", Stanton et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05945)
  Students often fail to match their teachers even when they have the capacity to, and better generalization does not require better fidelity. The experiments are mostly image classification, with one text task. Use for: separating fidelity from task quality.
- [Paper: "The False Promise of Imitating Proprietary LLMs", Gudibande et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15717)
  Imitation models rated close to ChatGPT by crowdworkers closed little of the gap on targeted benchmarks. Use for: the imitation gap and how human preference ratings hid it.
- [Paper: "Improved Knowledge Distillation via Teacher Assistant", Mirzadeh et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03393)
  Student quality falls when the gap between teacher and student grows too large, and an intermediate-sized assistant recovers some of it; the gains in its vision experiments are small. Use for: the capacity gap.
- [Paper: "On the Efficacy of Knowledge Distillation", Cho and Hariharan, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01348)
  More accurate teachers do not always make better students, and stopping the teacher early can help. Use for: a second account of the capacity gap.
- [Paper: "Subliminal Learning: Language models transmit behavioral traits via hidden signals in data", Cloud et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14805)
  A student trained on number sequences from a teacher picked up the teacher's traits, when both shared a base model. Use for: what filtering the data cannot remove.

### Sizing

- [Paper: "Distillation Scaling Laws", Busbridge et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08606)
  A scaling law for student quality as a function of student size, distillation tokens and teacher quality, and when distillation beats supervised training of the same student. Also reports the best distillation temperature was 1 in its setup (appendix G.2). Use for: stage 7 from the source, and the counterpoint to a temperature above 1 on an LLM.

### Licensing and terms

- [Terms: "Terms of Use", OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)
  OpenAI's consumer terms for users outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK, which list using output to develop models that compete with OpenAI among the things a user may not do. Use for: what a hosted provider's terms say about training on its outputs.
- [License: "Llama 3.1 Community License Agreement", Meta](https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_1/LICENSE)
  An open-weights license that allows using the model's outputs to improve another model, on condition that the resulting model's name begins with "Llama" when it is distributed. Use for: what an open model's license says about training on its outputs.

## Gaps

- Thinking Machines' post on on-policy distillation is widely cited for its cost comparison with RL, but its site refuses scripted and in-app reads, so no number from it is used here. Stage 5 rests on the GKD, MiniLLM and Qwen3 papers instead.
- Terms of use and licenses change without a version a lesson can pin. Lesson 0013 quotes them as read when this track was written; recheck the source before relying on it.
